Systems

A tool earns its place.

She looks for better tools where the day actually lives: mail, scheduling, and tasks. The point is fewer places to check, and a list that matches the real priorities.

Mail, scheduling, tasks

These three should agree with each other. She will move the work onto tools that fit the way the day runs. She will replace a stack of overlapping tools. She will not add a second system that someone has to maintain beside the first.

Whether a new tool belongs

A new tool has to earn its place. That includes new AI tools, the ones that draft, sort, or summarize. She does not collect them. She asks three questions.

Does it return hours to the principal? Does a person still make the decision? Can the office explain it on a calm day? If it fails those questions, the tool stays out. The principal's focus remains the product.

This judgment sits inside the practice, next to the gate and the room. It is part of how attention is spent.

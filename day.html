The day

An order, not a pile of requests.

She starts with the hours that already have a claim. New requests come after that, and only some of them come through. The room and the systems belong to the same day.

Gate

She keeps the calendar, the mail, the scheduling, and the travel in one view. She arranges travel around the work that has to happen in person. The itinerary and the buffers are part of the gate, because a badly planned trip spends the hours the practice is here to protect.

Priorities sit where they can be seen. A new request has to name what it will displace. If it cannot, it waits, or it stops.

People still receive an answer. Gatekeeping is a courtesy with a spine: a clear yes when the work belongs to the day, and a clear no when it would pull the principal off the product.

In the meeting

She sits in some company meetings. She will say what should change in the work, in the offer, and in the way attention is spent.

She can interview candidates. She can give an executive feedback on performance. The aim is a sentence the room can use, spoken plainly.

Customers

An inquiry gets a reply that names the next step. Onboarding is a sequence: what she needs from the person, what that person will receive, and when the first real piece of work starts.

A complaint gets one calm owner. That person holds the facts and the thread until the matter is closed, and does not ask anyone to tell the story twice. The manner stays steady when the other person is not.
